# Asset quality and current sources

Read when choosing or changing icons, graphics, images, motion, music, voice or effects. Reuse the UX Quality three-option decision record for uncertain choices; do not create a second voting loop.

## Choose and inspect

- **Icons:** prefer the established coherent family or appropriate native set. Compare uncertain families by coverage, meaning at actual size, optical weight, filled/outline consistency, accessibility, theming and license. Label unfamiliar actions. Avoid mixing unrelated sets or substituting random emoji for controls.
- **Graphics:** inspect rendered output at intended size/density, crop, transparency, color and light/dark background. Check compression, legibility, artifacts and stylistic fit. Choose useful quality within measured load/decode/memory budgets; maximum resolution is not automatically better.
- **Audio:** audition in the product at representative volume/output conditions, including repetition. Check clarity, noise, clipping/distortion, clicks at cuts, loop seams, onset latency, abrupt tails, loudness consistency, masking of speech/important cues and fatigue. Match voice/music/effects to intent; no universal codec, bitrate or loudness number without target-platform evidence.
- Respect mute, volume and platform interruption/audio-focus behavior. Where relevant, separate music/effects/dialogue controls and offer mono support, captions or equivalent visual/haptic cues. Essential information must survive sound off; decorative sounds must not harass users.
- Licensed libraries, native assets and custom/generated work are candidates, not automatic winners. Check source, version, usage/redistribution rights and required attribution before adoption. Record asset IDs/paths, modifications and rights evidence. Competitor screenshots are references, not shipping assets. Do not buy or accept new terms without authority.

Missing playback/render tools: keep quality unverified and identify the exact audition/inspection needed. Metadata, marketing copy and an attractive preview do not prove the delivered asset works in context.

## Research starting points

Checked 4 October 2026. Recheck the applicable source/version when making a current decision; these links are starting points, not a fixed vendor shortlist.

- [Nielsen's usability heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/): understandable, consistent, controllable workflows with useful feedback and recovery.
- [W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/): web accessibility criteria; distinguish measured conformance from a checklist claim.
- [Microsoft icon guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/develop/ui/controls/icons): platform-specific icon use. Research equivalent official guidance for the actual target.
- [Xbox audio accessibility](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/105): distinct volume controls, speech/cue clarity and relevant mono/pause behavior for games.

These sources inform the method. They do not prove a selected product, sound pack or design is best; that needs the actual comparison and inspection.
